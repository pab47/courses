    Autonomous Mobile Robots Bootcamp¶

    By Pranav A. Bhounsule · pranav@uic.edu

    An example of a sample outcome of the bootcamp.

    Purpose¶

    The purpose of this module is two-fold: first, to learn about autonomous mobile robot navigation, and second, to practice the workflow of taking an idea from concept, to simulation, to hardware.

    The module has two stages:

    • Stage 1 — you learn autonomous mobile robot navigation concepts and implement control in simulation. Simulation lets you try many ideas quickly and cheaply, and stress-test an idea under a variety of conditions before committing to it.
    • Stage 2 — you take an idea that worked in simulation to hardware, where the testing you already did in simulation greatly increases the chance of success.

    Stage 1 is self-paced and done at home. Stage 2 takes place at the University — typically over a weekend — where you implement your controller on real hardware.

    Outcomes¶

    At the end of these exercises, students will be able to:

    1. (Understand) Explain how common autonomous-navigation frameworks — heuristic controllers, artificial potential fields, timed elastic bands, the dynamic window approach, and model predictive control — decide how a robot should move to reach a goal while avoiding obstacles.
    2. (Understand) Describe how lidar and RGB-D cameras sense a robot's surroundings, and what data each sensor returns.
    3. (Understand) Explain how mecanum wheels combine four independently driven wheels to produce omni-directional motion.
    4. (Apply) Implement a go-to-goal controller with obstacle avoidance in simulation, using lidar and/or RGB-D camera data.
    5. (Analyze) Compare the performance of a heuristic controller against a principled controller (DWA, artificial potential field, timed elastic band, or MPC) across simple and BARN benchmark environments.
    6. (Create) Design, build, and deploy a working autonomous-navigation controller — from concept, through simulation, to real hardware.
