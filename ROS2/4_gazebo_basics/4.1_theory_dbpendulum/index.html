4.1 Theory: dbpendulum¶

The next two packages, 4.2 dbpendulum and 4.3 dbpendulum_ctrl, are ROS 2 / Gazebo implementations of math taught in Lec09–Lec11 and Lec17a–Lec17d of tiny.cc/robotics24. Watch these first — the Gazebo/ROS 2 versions on the following pages will make a lot more sense once you've seen the plain-Python derivation of the same dynamics and control law.

Lec09–Lec11 — Euler-Lagrange Dynamics of a Double Pendulum¶

Lec09 introduces the Euler-Lagrange method on a simple projectile, Lec10 covers the symbolic computation needed to apply it to a multi-link system, and Lec11 uses both to derive the double pendulum's full equations of motion — the same m1, m2, I1, I2, c1, c2, l model parameterized in dbpendulum's URDF. On the 4.2 dbpendulum page you don't need to code any of that derivation yourself: give Gazebo the masses, inertias, and geometry via <inertial> tags, and its physics engine integrates the same dynamics Lec09–11 derive by hand. 4.3 dbpendulum_ctrl is where those equations resurface explicitly, inside the controller.

Lec17a–Lec17d — Nonlinear Control of Manipulators¶

Lec17a introduces why a double pendulum (or any multi-link arm) needs nonlinear control, not simple PD; Lec17b works through a simpler, critical-damping-based special case; Lec17c derives feedback linearization for set-point (go-to-and-hold) control; and Lec17d extends that to trajectory tracking — the exact controller ported into 4.3 dbpendulum_ctrl's control node (see that page's Design notes for the M/C/G terms taken directly from Lec17d's derivation). Lec17a–c share the same code download as Lec17d.
